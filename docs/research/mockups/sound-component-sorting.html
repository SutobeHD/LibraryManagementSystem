Sort by sound components — wireframe

Planned UI for Option A of the research doc sound-component-sorting (plan revision 5): bass, hats and song-structure values plus "sort by similarity", all without a new dependency. Track names are placeholders. Numbers in cells are illustrative; the ranges and costs in the notes are measured on the real library.

Collection with component columns switched on

New columns live in the existing column picker, grouped by part (Bass, Kick, Höhen / Hats, Aufbau), and are hidden by default. They load only when one of them is visible. Cells show nicht analysiert before a scan; columns that passed only the "coarse" stability bar carry a marker. Sorting puts empty cells last.

Measured column set: values come from each track's main sections (Rekordbox phrases). New in revision 5, ≤ 33 ms extra per track: kick pitch (0.94), off-beat vs rolling bass (0.98), one-note bass (0.89), bass hits per beat, low-end weight, tonal vs noisy bass; structure: big drop, longest stretch without the main part, bars left after the last drop (0.93 across copies). Sub share, bass between kicks and bass note are stable across sections (0.94-0.98), so are 16th share (0.92) and top-end energy, 5-12 kHz (0.86). Off-beat hats are marked grob (0.84); two more optional columns, hats per beat and "Offbeat lang ausklingend", carry the same grob marker (0.80 / 0.82). On-beat hats are left out: that position measures clap and kick, not hats. Structure values come from Rekordbox's own phrase analysis: across 92 title-matched copies intro, entry and drop count agree within one bar in 87-97 % (exactly in 83-87 %); outro length is coarser and marked grob. Tracks Rekordbox has no phrases for (21 of 4765) show "—" in the structure columns.
2

Right-click → "Nach Ähnlichkeit sortieren"

One entry with eight kinds. Kick compares the kick's sound, pitch and tail; Groove compares the rhythm of bass and hats (coarse); Klang gesamt combines bass and top-end sound. Bass compares the bass sound on a pitch-relative scale (a transposed copy still matches) plus the bass line; "mit Kick" includes the kick. Hats (Klang) compares the high-end timbre and decay; Hat-Pattern compares where in the bar the hats fall (a coarser measure). Aufbau compares the phrase build-up within the same Rekordbox mood.

4

Settings → Analysis: two scans

Nothing runs on its own. The structure scan reads Rekordbox's phrase data only (no audio decode); the audio scan analyses the main sections of every track in the background. Both can be cancelled and resume where they stopped; values appear while they run.

Structure tooltip + family filter

Hovering the structure bar shows the phrase sequence in bars. Families are simple rules (number of breaks × when the main part starts), not a learned clustering — the measurement found no stable natural clusters.

Not part of this plan

Measured this time, still left out

  • Similar melodies: the right track comes first in about one case in three (chance: under 1 %). A transposed melody is recognised. Too rough to ship as it is.
  • How melodic / melody type: only slightly better than chance against your own "Melodic" tag; lead versus pad cannot be told apart yet.
  • Your own sound classes (bells and others): the mechanism works for broad classes, not yet shown for a sound inside one genre. It needs your example tracks.
Notes and sound fingerprints would need one small extra library (16 MB) and about 3 CPU hours for the whole library; no 2 GB GPU package. The next step here is your example tracks, not more code.

Dropped after measurement — confirmed 2026-11-22

  • On-beat hats: on the beat the high band is mostly clap and kick, so the value says nothing about hats. Left out.
  • One combined "same hats" score: hat sound and hat pattern are independent, so they are two separate entries. The pattern entry is the rougher one.
  • "Open hats": the value also rises with rides, noise and long synth sounds, so the column is called "Offbeat lang ausklingend" and marked grob.
  • Tonality column: stable, but it cannot tell a lead from a pad.
